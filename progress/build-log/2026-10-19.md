## Rules: the 100-page agreement's misses and British spellings

Request: improve the deterministic rules (`rules-v1`) for the misses on `15-limited-partnership-agreement-100-pages.docx` in the production run above, cut the British and legal spelling false alarms, and keep the offline eval at least as good, without tuning to that one file.

Causes and changes (`src/review/checks.ts`, `src/review/sections.ts`):
- **Conflicts worded differently.** The conflict rule only compared sentences with at least 80% of their words in common. "fund each capital call within 10 Business Days after receipt of a Capital Call Notice" and "an Investor that is excused ... shall nevertheless fund its share within fifteen (15) Business Days of the Capital Call Notice" share far less; so do the two term clauses, one of which adds "subject to two one-year extensions". The numerals were already read correctly ("(15) Business Days"). New: two obligations also conflict when they set the same kind of period (same unit) for the same action (the verb after shall/must/will, skipping adverbs) measured from the same event (the words after "after/of/from/following", without "receipt of" and articles), with different numbers. A spelled-out number in front of a bracketed numeral ("fifteen (15)") no longer counts as different wording. Conflicts about "a term of ..." are titled "Conflicting term lengths".
- **Exhibit K.** References to an exhibit and a schedule with the same letter share a key, and "Schedule K-1" (the US partnership tax form) was read as "Schedule K", reported missing, and so hid the missing "Exhibit K". "Schedule K-1" is no longer read as a reference to this document.
- **Spelling.** A word the dictionary rejects is now accepted when it is a known prefix (un, non, re, pre, co, sub, inter, over, under, counter, post, multi) plus a known word (unaudited), a party noun ending in -ee/-or whose stem is a word (transferee, transferor, pledgee), or a British -lful/-lment form (wilful, instalment, fulfilment); "-isational" is matched to "-izational" (organisational). "per annum", "inter alia", "pari passu" and "clawback" join the legal word list. Splitting into any two dictionary words was tried and dropped: it accepted "withold", "concensus", "relevent" and "publically".
- Not changed: the management fee (2.0% in Article 9 vs 1.75% in Schedule B) and governing law (Delaware recital vs New York) conflicts stay with the semantic reviewer. The fee rate appears once as a sentence and once as a bare table cell, and the governing-law pair needs to know that the law named in a recital and in a governing-law clause should agree; no rule found was both generic and precise.
